SIP over TLS uses port 5061 (RFC 3261). SRTP (RFC 3711) protects media. SDES (RFC 4568) puts the key in SDP. DTLS-SRTP (RFC 5764) exchanges the key in DTLS and is what WebRTC uses. RFC 5630 explains why a SIPS URI is not, by itself, a promise that every hop is encrypted. Toll fraud is unsolicited calling placed through an open trunk. Scanning is unsolicited SIP probing. Controls are authentication, TLS, a closed dial plan, and not exposing UDP 5060 to the Internet.

    Failure tree

    International calls at 03:00 that nobody placed

    Checks. CDR source, dial-peer or route pattern, and whether 5060 is reachable from outside.

    Likely cause. An open SIP listener or a class of service that permits premium destinations.

    Fix. Close the listener, require credentials, and deny premium routes for phones that should not place them.

    SRTP fails with 488

    Checks. The keying method in SDP: crypto (SDES) or a DTLS fingerprint.

    Likely cause. One side offers SDES and the other offers only DTLS-SRTP.

    Fix. Pick one keying method per trunk.

    SIPS on the URI, cleartext on a hop

    Checks. TLS on every connection, not only the scheme.

    Likely cause. RFC 5630: the SIPS scheme does not magically encrypt a hop that was configured as UDP.

    Fix. Configure TLS on the trunk. Then check the capture.
