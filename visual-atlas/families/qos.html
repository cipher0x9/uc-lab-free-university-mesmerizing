Differentiated services mark packets with a DSCP. Expedited Forwarding (RFC 3246) is DSCP 46 and is the PHB used for voice. RFC 4594 assigns telephony to EF and signaling to CS5. Cisco enterprise QoS baselines have long used EF for voice, AF41 (DSCP 34) for interactive video, and CS3 (DSCP 24) for call signaling. Confirm the current Cisco QoS design guide before you copy CS3 into a policy. A trust boundary is the place you decide which marks to believe. LLQ is the Cisco scheduler that serves a priority queue for that EF traffic.

    Failure tree

    Voice is marked EF on the phone and AF11 on the WAN

    Checks. The mark on the phone port and on the WAN interface.

    Likely cause. The access port does not trust the phone, or a later hop rewrites DSCP.

    Fix. Trust the voice VLAN only, and stop rewriting EF.

    Priority queue drops even at low call counts

    Checks. How many calls, which codec, and the priority bandwidth.

    Likely cause. The queue is smaller than the calls you admitted. A G.711 call is 64 kbit/s of payload plus RTP/UDP/IP overhead.

    Fix. Admit calls to match the queue. Do not invent a per-call overhead number you have not measured.

    Signaling lags and media is fine

    Checks. DSCP on SIP versus RTP.

    Likely cause. Media is EF and signaling was left at 0, or the reverse of your policy.

    Fix. Mark signaling on purpose. RFC 4594 says CS5. Cisco baselines have used CS3. Pick one and document it.
