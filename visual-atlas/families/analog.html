FXS and FXO describe which side provides battery and dial tone. An FXS port feeds a telephone. An FXO port faces the line and draws battery from the far side. Loop start signals seizure by closing the loop. Ground start is used on trunks so both ends can detect glare. Tip and ring are the two wires of the pair.

    Failure tree

    No dial tone at the phone

    Checks. Is the pair on an FXS port? Is the phone on-hook voltage present?

    Likely cause. The port is FXO, or the pair is open.

    Fix. Move the phone to FXS. FXO is the trunk side and will not feed a phone.

    Trunks glare (both ends seize)

    Checks. Is the trunk loop start on a two-way trunk?

    Likely cause. Loop start cannot reliably show who seized first.

    Fix. Use ground start or a supervised digital trunk for two-way trunks.

    Call does not clear when the caller hangs up

    Checks. Disconnect supervision timing on the FXO port.

    Likely cause. The far end opened the loop too briefly, or supervision is disabled.

    Fix. Enable disconnect supervision and match the open interval the carrier sends.
